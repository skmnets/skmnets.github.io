I am a PhD student in Industrial Engineering and Management Systems at the University of Central Florida (UCF) and a Graduate Research Assistant in the Complex Adaptive Systems Laboratory (CASL), advised by Prof. Ivan Garibay.

My research lies at the intersection of graph machine learning, graph limits, network science, and mathematical machine learning. I am particularly interested in mathematical and population-level descriptions of how information is routed, transformed, and recovered in learning systems defined on networks.

Before joining UCF IEMS, I completed an M.S. in Mathematical Science at UCF, where I was a recipient of the UCF Dean’s Fellowship, and a BS–MS Dual Degree in Mathematical Sciences at IISER Kolkata, supported by the INSPIRE Scholarship for Higher Education (INSPIRE-SHE) from the Department of Science and Technology, Government of India.

Research

Graph Limits for Task-Conditioned Communication

Graph neural networks communicate along edges, but the usefulness of a communication route depends on the task being solved. We study how message-passing structure should adapt to a downstream objective and how task-dependent communication patterns can be compared across graph resolutions. This is addressed through graph-limit representations that provide a common population coordinate system, together with task-conditioned edge sensitivities that identify whether strengthening or weakening a route locally improves the objective.

Sparse Neural Routing and Kernel Limits

What mathematical structure survives when neural networks become increasingly sparse as their width grows? This work develops continuum limits for pruned neural networks and shows that normalized routing structure, rather than raw connection density, governs meaningful NNGP and NTK behavior across sparsity regimes. The analysis provides a way to characterize when sparse architectures with very different densities induce the same limiting computation.

Information Horizons in Graph Diffusion

How much structural information remains recoverable as a graph is progressively corrupted by a diffusion process? We study this question using graphons, spectral structure, and size-aware signal-to-noise coordinates. The resulting analysis identifies distinct information horizons for detection, consistency, and exact recovery, and characterizes how reverse diffusion changes after information about the original source structure is no longer statistically identifiable.

Collaborative Research

Collaborative work also considers problems in generative modeling and computational biology, including motif-aware discrete diffusion for molecular generation and higher-order set models for combinatorial gene-regulatory inference.

Selected Research

  1. LoG 2026
    Normalized Routing Limits for Pruned Neural Networks: NNGP and NTK across Density Regimes
    Siddhi Kanta Mishra and Ivan Garibay
    Learning on Graphs Conference (LoG), 2026
    Accepted at the Learning on Graphs Conference (LoG 2026); forthcoming in PMLR.
    Abs Bib

    A binary pruning mask encodes which synaptic connections are retained or removed in a neural network and can be viewed as the biadjacency matrix of a directed bipartite graph. When retained density vanishes, its raw step graphon collapses in L¹, even though each neuron may still aggregate a growing, structured neighborhood. We show that under local retained-fan-in scaling, the network instead sees the row-Markov routing operator P = D⁻¹M, which records the relative allocation of retained inputs. For two-hidden-layer networks with independent row-constrained block masks and growing minimum fan-in, we derive deterministic finite-dimensional NNGP covariance and initialization-time NTK limits across fixed- and vanishing-density regimes, prove density invariance, identify block-mass adjoints governing backward sensitivity, and resolve forward–backward weight reuse via Gaussian-conditioning decoupling.

  2. LoG 2026
    Community Information Horizons in Discrete Graph Diffusion: A Graphon Analysis
    Siddhi Kanta Mishra and Ivan Garibay
    Learning on Graphs Conference (LoG), 2026
    Accepted at the Learning on Graphs Conference (LoG 2026); forthcoming in PMLR.
    Abs Bib

    At what noise level does a corrupted graph stop identifying the structure that generated it? We show that the answer depends on graph size, not diffusion time alone. Under binary edge refresh, every dense probability graphon follows the exact population trajectory Wₜ = ρ + ᾱₜ(W₀ - ρ). To determine when a finite graph can still resolve the contracted structure, we specialize to stochastic block graphons and define the size-aware structural signal-to-noise ratio zₜ. This separates three recovery regimes: weak detection begins at zₜ = 1, consistency requires zₜ → ∞, and exact recovery occurs at zₜ² ≍ log n. Reverse diffusion paths expose an alignment switch: crossing the source-information horizon causes reverse chains to lose alignment with the specific source partition while synthesizing fresh, coherent population-level community modes.

  3. Preprint
    Learning Where to Communicate in GNNs: Task-Conditioned Edge Scores Across Graph Sizes
    Siddhi Kanta Mishra and Ivan Garibay
    Manuscript under review at ICLR 2027, 2026
    Manuscript under review at ICLR 2027
    Abs Bib

    Graph neural networks succeed by moving information across edges, but which communication routes are actually useful for a given task? We develop a task-conditioned edge score that describes whether strengthening or weakening a route would locally improve the objective. The score couples the representation difference exchanged across a route with a backward sensitivity describing how that exchange affects the task, gated by activation derivatives. This allows useful heterophilous as well as homophilous communication. Graph limits provide a common population coordinate system for comparing these scores across resolutions. For nonlinear residual message passing with symmetric mean-normalized difference aggregation, we derive the exact score and prove finite-horizon consistency of topology learning. On METR-LA, scores from independently trained smaller models guide useful interventions on a larger frozen predictor.

  4. ICMLW 2026
    MoCDiff: Efficient Motif-Constrained Discrete Diffusion for Molecule Generation
    Tasfia Nuzhat Ornee, Mohammad Jahid Ibna Basher, Siddhi Kanta Mishra, and 3 more authors
    In ICML 2026 Workshop on Generative and Agentic AI for Biology, 2026
    Abs Bib

    Molecular graph generation requires models that capture chemical structure while producing valid, diverse, and novel molecules within practical sampling budgets. We present MoCDiff, a Motif-Constrained masked Discrete Diffusion framework built on two complementary components: mSENT, a motif-aware graph-to-sequence tokenizer that biases graph traversal toward chemically coherent substructures while preserving exact graph decodability, and an optimized constrained sampler with inexact ALM updates, conditionally adaptive penalties, and lazy enforcement. On QM9, mSENT raises validity from 85.3% to 90.5% and uniqueness from 75.0% to 97.7% over standard SENT, while achieving a 1.62× speedup and 1.7× higher accepted-sample throughput over baseline CDD.
