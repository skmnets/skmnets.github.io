I am a Doctoral Researcher in Industrial Engineering and Management Systems at the University of Central Florida (UCF) and a Graduate Research Assistant in the Complex Adaptive Systems Laboratory (CASL), advised by Prof. Ivan Garibay.

Previously, I earned an MS in Mathematics from UCF (2022–2025) supported by the UCF Dean’s Fellowship, and a BS–MS Dual Degree in Mathematical Sciences from the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Kolkata (2016–2021) supported by the INSPIRE Fellowship from the Department of Science and Technology, Government of India.

My research lies at the intersection of theoretical machine learning, graph limits, network science, and generative modeling, focusing on continuous formulations that explain and guide learning on discrete, sparse, and multi-scale network structures.

Research Themes

  • Task-Conditioned Communication & Graph Limits in GNNs: Investigating which communication routes actually benefit a graph neural network solving a downstream task. I derive explicit, activation-gated task derivatives for population-level communication ($S_W$), demonstrating that task alignment can favor heterophilous as well as homophilous communication. By viewing graphons as population coordinate systems, I prove finite-horizon consistency of topology learning across resolutions and show that scores from smaller models guide effective topology interventions on larger, frozen predictors (evaluated on synthetic relational tasks and traffic networks like METR-LA).
  • Normalized Routing Limits for Pruned Neural Networks: Developing parameterization-faithful continuum limits for width-growing sparse neural networks under aggressive pruning. While raw adjacency graphons collapse in $L^1$ under vanishing edge density, local retained-fan-in scaling exposes a stable row-Markov routing operator ($P = D^{-1}M$). For two-hidden-layer networks, I establish deterministic finite-dimensional NNGP covariance and NTK limits across fixed- and vanishing-density regimes, prove density invariance, identify block-mass adjoints ($B^{(2)\dagger}$) governing backward sensitivity, and resolve forward–backward weight reuse through a Gaussian-conditioning decoupling argument.
  • Community Information Horizons in Discrete Graph Diffusion: Establishing exact continuous graphon trajectories ($W_t = \rho + \bar{\alpha}_t(W_0 - \rho)$) and mode-contraction dynamics under discrete binary edge refresh. By introducing a size-aware structural signal-to-noise ratio ($z_t = \frac{\bar{\alpha}_t \Delta \sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{p_t(1 - p_t)}}$), I characterize three fundamental recovery regimes—weak detection ($z_t = 1$), consistency ($z_t \to \infty$), and exact recovery ($z_t^2 \asymp \log n$). This analysis reveals an alignment switch, where reverse diffusion chains cross the source-information horizon, lose alignment with the original source partition, and synthesize fresh, coherent population-level community modes.
  • Combinatorial Gene Regulation via Exact Set Discovery: Reframing gene regulatory network (GRN) inference from traditional pairwise edge prediction into an exact combinatorial regulator set discovery problem. I developed a two-stage filter-and-refine pipeline using context-aware attention retrieval followed by Residual HOS2—a residual high-order set model that captures non-additive transcription factor interactions on top of a decomposable pairwise base.
  • Motif-Constrained Molecular Diffusion (MoCDiff): Addressing tokenization and sampling bottlenecks in discrete molecular diffusion. We develop mSENT, a motif-aware graph-to-sequence serialization policy that preserves rigid chemical substructures (rings, aromatic cores) as contiguous token spans while maintaining full exact graph decodability, paired with an optimized constrained diffusion sampler (inexact ALM, lazy projection) to drastically improve sampling throughput.

selected publications

  1. LoG 2026
    Normalized Routing Limits for Pruned Neural Networks: NNGP and NTK across Density Regimes
    Siddhi Kanta Mishra and Ivan Garibay
    Learning on Graphs Conference (LoG), 2026
    forthcoming (Oct 2026)
    Abs Bib

    A binary pruning mask encodes which synaptic connections are retained or removed in a neural network and can be viewed as the biadjacency matrix of a directed bipartite graph. When retained density vanishes, its raw step graphon collapses in L1, even though each neuron may still aggregate a growing, structured neighborhood. We show that under local retained-fan-in scaling, the network instead sees the row-Markov routing operator P = D^-1M, which records the relative allocation of retained inputs. For two-hidden-layer networks with independent row-constrained block masks and growing minimum fan-in, we derive deterministic finite-dimensional NNGP covariance and initialization-time NTK limits across fixed- and vanishing-density regimes, prove density invariance, identify block-mass adjoints governing backward sensitivity, and resolve forward-backward weight reuse via Gaussian-conditioning decoupling.

  2. LoG 2026
    Community Information Horizons in Discrete Graph Diffusion: A Graphon Analysis
    Siddhi Kanta Mishra and Ivan Garibay
    Learning on Graphs Conference (LoG), 2026
    forthcoming (Oct 2026)
    Abs Bib

    At what noise level does a corrupted graph stop identifying the structure that generated it? We show that the answer depends on graph size, not diffusion time alone. Under binary edge refresh, every dense probability graphon follows the exact population trajectory Wt = rho + alpha_t(W0 - rho). To determine when a finite graph can still resolve the contracted structure, we specialize to stochastic block graphons and define the size-aware structural signal-to-noise ratio z_t. This separates three recovery regimes: weak detection begins at z_t = 1, consistency requires z_t -> infty, and exact recovery occurs at z_t^2   log n. Reverse diffusion paths expose an alignment switch: crossing the source-information horizon causes reverse chains to lose alignment with the specific source partition while synthesizing fresh, coherent population-level community modes.

  3. ICMLW 2026
    MoCDiff: Efficient Motif-Constrained Discrete Diffusion for Molecule Generation
    Tasfia Nuzhat Ornee, Mohammad Jahid Ibna Basher, Siddhi Kanta Mishra, and 3 more authors
    In ICML 2026 Workshop on Generative and Agentic AI for Biology, 2026
    Abs Bib

    Molecular graph generation requires models that capture chemical structure while producing valid, diverse, and novel molecules within practical sampling budgets. We present MoCDiff, a Motif-Constrained masked Discrete Diffusion framework built on two complementary components: mSENT, a motif-aware graph-to-sequence tokenizer that biases graph traversal toward chemically coherent substructures while preserving exact graph decodability, and an optimized constrained sampler with inexact ALM updates, conditionally adaptive penalties, and lazy enforcement. On QM9, mSENT raises validity from 85.3% to 90.5% and uniqueness from 75.0% to 97.7% over standard SENT, while achieving a 1.62x speedup and 1.7x higher accepted-sample throughput over baseline CDD.

  4. Preprint
    Learning Where to Communicate in GNNs: Task-Conditioned Edge Scores Across Graph Sizes
    Siddhi Kanta Mishra and Ivan Garibay
    Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027, 2026
    Abs Bib

    Graph neural networks succeed by moving information across edges, but which communication routes are actually useful for a given task? We develop a task-conditioned edge score that describes whether strengthening or weakening a route would locally improve the objective. The score couples the representation difference exchanged across a route with a backward sensitivity describing how that exchange affects the task, gated by activation derivatives. This allows useful heterophilous as well as homophilous communication. Graph limits provide a common population coordinate system for comparing these scores across resolutions. For nonlinear residual message passing with symmetric mean-normalized difference aggregation, we derive the exact score and prove finite-horizon consistency of topology learning. On METR-LA, scores from independently trained smaller models guide useful interventions on a larger frozen predictor.
